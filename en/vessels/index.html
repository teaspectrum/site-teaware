Gaiwan

A three-part vessel with a lid, bowl and saucer, used both for brewing and for drinking. A white porcelain gaiwan is neutral, easy to observe and a practical tool for learning and comparing teas.

Kettle

A kettle or heating vessel for brewing water. Stable temperature control matters more than expensive materials; safety depends on the material, heat source and handling.

Pitcher

A vessel that receives brewed tea and divides it among cups so each cup has a similar strength. Capacity, pouring and cut-off matter; glass or white porcelain makes the liquor easy to observe.

Tea cup

A small cup for drinking tea. The rim, body and material affect aroma and the first impression in the mouth; a smooth rim and stable foot matter more than decoration.

Teapot

A brewing vessel with a spout, handle and lid. Materials and shapes vary widely, and heat retention is usually better than a gaiwan. Pouring, capacity and comfort matter more than appearance.
